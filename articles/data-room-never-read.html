They Asked for the Data Room Three Times. They Never Read It.

Jun 1, 2026 · 5 min read

You spent 40 hours building it. The financial models, the unit economics breakdown, the customer cohort analysis, the legal folder with every contract properly named and dated. You shared the link. They confirmed receipt. Three weeks later, they asked for it again. You re-sent. Then a third request — "sorry, the link expired."

They passed two weeks after that. No specific feedback. "Just not the right fit for the portfolio right now."

They never opened it.

The data room as a signal, not a resource

Here's what most founders don't understand about data room requests: in early-stage deals, asking for the data room is often a delay tactic, not a due diligence step. A VC who has conviction will ask pointed questions — specific numbers, specific metrics, specific references. A VC who is on the fence asks for the data room because it buys time without requiring them to say no.

Requesting the data room is the polite way of saying "we haven't decided whether we're interested yet."

This isn't always true — later-stage deals, larger checks, and institutional LPs require real diligence. But at pre-seed and seed, a data room request before a partner meeting is often theater.

How to respond without wasting weeks

Give them the data room — but gate it. Use a tool that shows you who opens it, which files they view, and how long they spend. Notion, Docsend, and similar platforms track this. If the room gets shared and nobody opens anything within 72 hours, you have a signal. Follow up with something specific: "I noticed you haven't had a chance to look at the financials — happy to walk you through the key numbers in a 20-minute call."

This reframes the conversation from document exchange to live qualification. It also saves you from the three-request cycle.

What a serious DD request looks like

You'll know when they're actually doing diligence. It looks like:

  • Questions about specific line items in your model, not the model generally
  • Reference checks on your customers or early employees
  • A legal review request — usually means they've already decided internally
  • Follow-up questions within 48 hours of sharing materials
  • An intro to the operating partner or technical team

If none of that is happening, the data room is decorative. Keep your pipeline moving. The worst outcome in fundraising isn't a rejection — it's three months spent waiting on someone who was never going to say yes.
